V1 transport and the next proof-size target

The public Solana Devnet v1 cases complete authorization in 18.08 seconds
with 88 transactions and 22.34 seconds with 91 transactions under injected
packet loss and reordering. All 64 query predicates remain in the verifier.
The client submits each window as a QUIC batch. Every upload also checks
whether its bitmap is complete. The completing transaction verifies the
whole frame atomically. Confirmed account updates enable the next window.

The current signed packet limit is 4,096 bytes. Payload chunks use 3,584
bytes. The largest measured authorization remains within 1,400,000 CU.
There are fourteen ordered windows. The protected cache and accepted query
prefix survive delivery retries. The full temporary reserve is returned.

Byte accounting for the normal public v1 capsule

  Encoded delta capsule:             278,085 bytes.
  Global Merkle frontier estimate:    224,312 bytes.
  With embedded public preprocessing:   194,848 bytes.
  Payload-only v1 packet floors:      55 and 48 transactions.

These frontier totals describe prospective lossless encodings of this
fixed proof. Their packet floors divide by 4,096 and include zero framing
overhead. They are format-specific counts. A thirty-transaction target
therefore calls for a smaller proof representation in addition to v1
transport and removal of repeated authentication material.

A concrete next research path is transparent recursive compression of the
public capsule. The compression statement binds the request, authority and
parameter identity and requires acceptance by the strict inner verifier.
An external prover can receive that public capsule. The wallet retains
the Parano1d secret and its masking seed. The outer system needs quantum
knowledge extraction that recovers the inner capsule in the same adaptive
authorization experiment, with its extraction loss quantified. Privacy can
then be reduced to the inner capsule's witness-protection property because
the compressor receives only the already-public proof. This is the next
protocol research obligation.
