Atomic native responses and parallel quantum extraction
9 November 2026

The native proof uses one 512-bit response for every extension challenge
and one response for the complete 64-position subset and its grinding
predicate. Its resource bound applies to classical authorization requests
and coherent access to both hash components.

1. Native response grammar

Write H=SHA256||Keccak256 and N=2^512. Each observed message is appended
to the complete previous digest. Field sampling consumes all 64 bytes.
It reads their reverse byte order as a little-endian integer, reduces it
modulo p^8 and returns its eight radix-p digits. Its largest point mass
is ceil(N/p^8)/N. A field bad set of cardinality b consequently has
probability at most b*ceil(N/p^8)/N.

The query seal hashes the final polynomial and its full predecessor.
The candidate input is the seal digest followed by one reduced field
word. Its output is one integer y in [0,N). Accepting a witness requires
y mod 4096=0. The subset rank is floor(y/4096) mod C(4096,64). Thus the
source density is exactly the envelope in source-restoration.txt.
All trials are ordinary charged calls. A transcript with an exceptional
out-of-domain challenge is charged at the corresponding field flag.

Merkle inputs have fourteen-byte leaf and node tags. Their lengths are
two modulo four. The initial transcript input has length three modulo
four. Later inputs have length zero modulo four. A native field or query
response therefore has one typed input. The 40-byte capacity-seed input
is fixed separately. The initial context contains the request's second
32-byte digest. The field public values contain its first digest.

2. Rooted reconstruction and source flags

A collision-free database gives every full digest a unique recorded
preimage. Starting at a typed response input, follow complete predecessor
digests to recover its public instance and earlier responses. Expand the
recorded source roots to their fixed heights. Missing source positions
are zero-completed. The authenticated preprocessing word is public.
This gives the exact rooted prefix used by the source extractor.

A field flag is its finite exceptional response set. A query flag is a
passing grind and a subset contained in the prefix's good-position set
when source extraction fails. Prefix malformedness and missing final
verification pairs are covered by the hash recording term. Each
well-formed candidate has the conditional density supplied by the source
lemma, uniformly for every fixed earlier database and source word.

The bad property is the presence of at least one such flag for an
identifier outside the requested set J. An identifier is the full pair
of canonical request digests. It enters J before an authorization is
constructed. A terminal fresh accepted proof outside the bad property
provides an authority preimage by the source extraction lemma.

3. Global protection of a database prefix

For a parallel query tuple x, delete its addressed rows and fix the
remaining database. Collect all recorded digest labels and every typed
hash pointer in the remaining keys and in x. Add the fixed public seed
label and self-return exclusions. At most beta=8T+32 labels are needed.
This set is fixed throughout that tuple's update.

Replace the uniform compression reflection by the reflection using only
allowed labels and fixing each forbidden label. If a fraction f is
forbidden, the uniform vectors have inner product sqrt(1-f). Their
reflection difference is at most 2sqrt(f). The corresponding lookup
sandwiches differ by at most 4sqrt(f). Applying the scalar telescoping
identity to all physical calls gives global state error at most

    E_protect(T)=4T sqrt((8T+32)/N).

This comparison is made once for the whole experiment. It is added to
the terminal success amplitude after the transition bound.

Every already rooted dependency has an output in the forbidden set.
Its compressed row is fixed, including against deletion to the blank
symbol. A new row cannot complete an old missing dependency, since its
new output would have to equal a forbidden pointer. Thus each earlier
rooted prefix is invariant. For one parallel tuple, decompose into the
orthogonal fibers of the fixed rooted labels. On each fiber a new flag
can arise only at one of its addressed response rows. Its bad set depends
only on that row's new output and the fixed prefix.

These are the one-local recognizing sets of CFHL21, Definition 5.14.
The uniform distribution on allowed labels increases any density by at
most 1/(1-beta/N). The Frobenius-norm proof of CFHL21, Theorem 5.17,
applied to the restricted compression reflection therefore gives

    transition_s <= sqrt(10 sum_j k_sj kappa_j/(1-beta/N)).

The direct-sum fibers do not multiply this bound by their number. The
query response is one row, so its entire subset has the joint density.

4. Adaptive constructors and retained input exchanges

The witness-free simulator of simulation.txt uses a classical input
permutation whose endpoints are private leaf inputs. It fixes every
transcript, internal node and public preprocessing input. A constructor's
own identifier is already in J. Its transcript calls do not create a
fresh-identifier source flag.

A previous fresh prefix can be changed by a retained exchange only if it
uses an old endpoint or a new constructor label. For an old database of
at most T rows, a fresh actual endpoint has membership probability at
most T/p^20. A dummy endpoint conditioned on its digest has membership
probability at most 2TN/p^36. A fresh constructor digest hits one of the
old pointer labels with probability at most 2(T+v)/N. These bounds hold
on arbitrary coherent old states by conditioning on each deleted-row
fiber and applying the projector norm bound 2sqrt(probability).

There are at most v endpoints or labels in one attempt. Summing the two
comparison directions, raw guard projections and S adaptive attempts is
covered, for T>=v, by the following state-distance envelope:

    E_updates(S,T)=128 S(T+v) sqrt(v/p^20)
        +128 S v sqrt((T+v)/N)
        +128 S v sqrt(2(T+v)N/p^36).

The first term dominates the summed actual-endpoint bound
8Sv sqrt(T/p^20), since T>=v. The last two terms cover dummy endpoints
and all new-label tests directly. On the complementary fibers the
input exchanges preserve all previous fresh prefixes exactly. Pruning
and the algebraic-view hybrid are charged separately in eps_sim.
The comparison uses raw tapes and aborts the complete experiment on a
failed retained guard.

5. Recording and terminal extraction

Count every setup, constructor, projection and final recheck in T.
Ordinary compressed-oracle collision search has success amplitude at
most 2e(T+1)sqrt(10(T+1)/N)+sqrt(2/N), by CFHL21, Theorem 5.29.
Input-label exchanges preserve equality of recorded outputs. Constructor
calls are ordinary physical calls. The displayed expression therefore
continues to hold between their input-label permutations. Together with
at most 65536 terminal pair checks and fixed-row exclusions, use

    E_record(T)=32 sqrt((T+1)^3/N)+256/sqrt(N).

Measure the compressed database once at the end. Apply the final input
permutation, enumerate its rooted prefixes and run the source extractor.
Validate each returned authority preimage. The extractor has the original
quantum execution followed by deterministic classical list decoding.
Its complete classical decoding cost is part of the preimage adversary
B. It makes no additional quantum queries or rewinds.

6. Resource theorem

For batch s, let k_sj count its responses of type j. A_s is its logical
gate count and delta_s its logical depth. The declared batch response
price c_j requires sum_j k_sj*c_j <= A_s*delta_s. Let sum A_s<=G and
sum delta_s<=D. Cauchy-Schwarz gives

    sum_s sqrt(sum_j k_sj*kappa_j)
      <= sqrt(GD max_j(kappa_j/c_j)).

Put R=GD max_j(kappa_j/c_j), beta=8T+32 and beta<N. The native
same-run authorization reduction is

    Pr[ReleaseForge] <= Adv_pre(B)+eps_der+eps_rng+eps_sim(S,T)
       +(sqrt(10R/(1-beta/N))+E_protect(T)
          +E_updates(S,T)+E_record(T))^2.

The bound is capped at one. Projection from either hash component is
simulated by returning the complete hybrid response. Its price premise
uses the cheaper component interface. Classical attacks use the same
source recognizing sets with the ordinary union bound sum k_sj*kappa_j.

Reference: Chung, Fehr, Huang and Liao, On the Compressed-Oracle
Technique, and Post-Quantum Security of Proofs of Sequential Work,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11658, Theorems 5.7, 5.17 and 5.29.
