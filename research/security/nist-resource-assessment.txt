NIST-style Category 1 resource assessment
9 November 2026

The fixed external native-vault profile supports a conservative
preliminary NIST-style Category 1 classification under its documented
QROM, authority, derivation, generator, host and resource conditions.
At the AES-128 reference budget, the complete ideal proof-loss bound
is 0.325523273624783 when the amortized response price is 2^20
gate-depth. The separate primitive advantages must sum to less than
0.174476726375217.

Native fixed-prefix circuits now supply concrete resource evidence for
that price. Across the evaluated SHA-256 and Keccak constructions,
three seal samples and pruned output projections, the cheapest
conservative data-computation product is 3,014,449 gate-depth. This
includes an explicitly assessed one-bit phase interface. It is 2.875
times the selected price and 4.416 times the sufficient price 682672.

The exact QROM theorem, the evaluated direct-circuit model and the
broader cryptanalytic assumptions are stated separately below. This
is a completed preliminary resource assessment in the NIST style.
Alternate attack algorithms, circuit synthesis and memory tradeoffs
remain subjects of continued cryptanalysis.

1. Exact native theorem and Category 1 arithmetic

The implementation uses a 4096-position domain, degree-eight
challenges, 64 distinct queries, twenty salt words, twelve grinding
bits and Poseidon2 RF=8, RP=26. The source agreement threshold is 896.
The exact query-response density is approximately 2^-154.9410960693.
The field density is approximately 2^-176.2034372942.

For response types j and parallel batches s, compiler.txt gives the
transition amplitude sqrt(10 sum_j k_sj*kappa_j/(1-beta/N)), where
N=2^512 and beta=8T+32. Its protected-prefix construction, adaptive
updates, recording bound and witness-free simulator supply the finite
terms. Count setup, constructor, projection and terminal calls in T.

If sum_j k_sj*c_j <= A_s*delta_s for every batch, sum A_s <= G and
sum delta_s <= D, Cauchy-Schwarz gives the documented authorization bound

  Adv_pre(B)+eps_der+eps_rng+eps_sim
  +(sqrt(10*GD*max_j(kappa_j/c_j)/(1-beta/N))
      +E_protect+E_updates+E_record)^2.

The evaluator uses kappa_query+kappa_field and c=2^20. With GD=2^170,
D in {2^40,2^64,2^96} and at most 2^64 classical authorization requests,
its complete ideal bound is at most 0.325523273624783. At 2^143
classical gates its bound is 0.000254315057964 even with a unit
price per physical hash call. These values include the finite terms.

The smallest sufficient integer price for an ideal bound below one
half is 682672. The chosen price is supported by the independent
native circuit assessment below. The full authorization conclusion
also requires Adv_pre(B)+eps_der+eps_rng < 0.174476726375217, with the
complete deterministic extraction and candidate-validation work
included in B as specified in authorization.txt.

2. NIST reference and logical-gate vocabulary

NIST Category 1 compares computational resources with AES-128 key
search across relevant metrics. Its stated reference is
2^170/MAXDEPTH quantum gates or 2^143 classical gates, with example
depths 2^40, 2^64 and 2^96. NIST asks for conservative preliminary
classifications based on attack estimates [1].

For a retained binary Boolean DAG, let C be its live AND count plus
its live XOR count, and d its AND-depth. The assessed direct model
evaluates those nodes individually. Grant each AND and XOR unit cost
and all complemented edges zero cost. Then the complete logical gate
count is at least C and the logical depth at least d for that
realization. C*d is an optimistic resource proxy. It omits fanout,
workspace preparation, cleanup, measurement corrections and XOR depth.

For k independent closed calls, A_s >= k*C and delta_s >= d, so
A_s*delta_s >= k*C*d. This justifies the selected price within the
evaluated direct-call family. Calls split across scheduling intervals
must include their complete dependency computation in the accounting.

The four-T temporary-AND mapping supplies a separate Clifford+T
check. T-count times T-depth describes partial accounting in that
mapping. It is distinct from the all-gate AES reference. Counting only
AND nodes discards the live XOR work and is insufficient for the
all-gate comparison.

3. Native fixed-prefix and output-cone results

The candidate input is a fixed 64-byte seal followed by a canonical
four-byte field nonce. There are p=2130706433 canonical nonces,
approximately 2^30.9886846874. The counter uses 31 variable nonce
bits and a fixed zero top bit. It computes a superset of the canonical
domain and omits the range-check cost.

SHA-256 needs two padded compression blocks. The counter caches the
first block's classical midstate and retains the complete second-block
schedule, 64 rounds and digest feed-forward. It evaluates ripple
majority and Kogge-Stone carry constructions. Native Keccak-256 uses
one padded permutation with its 136-byte rate and 0x01 delimiter.

The DAG applies local constant propagation, complemented edges,
structural common-subexpression elimination and output-cone pruning.
It uses zero, all-0xff and deterministic SHA-512 seal samples. The
measured minima over those samples are

  Construction and projection     C         d       C*d

  Keccak full 256-bit output      141202      24      3388848
  Keccak native grind bits        135882      24      3261168
  Keccak one output bit           131367      24      3152808
  Keccak phase data dependencies  131063      23      3014449
  SHA ripple one output bit        92485    1497    138450045
  SHA prefix one output bit       220898    1141    252044618

The native grind is digest byte 30's low four bits and all of byte 31.
The one-bit interface is digest byte 31's low bit. SHA rows use the
same byte projections for a component-cost comparison. Full SHA
outputs and twelve-bit projections have larger products in these
constructions. The complete sample ranges are in the JSON assessment.

The theorem's component simulation grants full hybrid responses, so
the resource comparison uses the cheaper component or projection.
Summing the two component circuit costs would overprice that interface.
The native response lengths are 623, 192, 9036, 364, 128, 128, 128,
96 and 68 bytes. The present concrete specialization covers the
68-byte candidate and its selected output cones.

The counter validates 144 hashes against hashlib.sha256 and
Crypto.Hash.keccak, plus 48 full-symbolic Keccak padded messages.
This assessment independently rebuilt all nine sampled DAGs,
recounted every retained gate and depth, and checked 432 hashes.
This includes 288 additional nonce cases. Every comparison passed.

4. One-bit phase interface

A phase query implements |x> -> (-1)^h(x)|x>. The assessment includes
the usual clean output-XOR implementation with phase kickback and a
second construction that strips the final XOR cone.

Expand that cone over F2, cancel repeated terms, and use

  (-1)^(a XOR b) = (-1)^a * (-1)^b.

At the remaining frontier, an AND contributes a CZ phase on its two
controls and a variable contributes Z. Compute their data dependencies,
apply the phases and clean the work registers. Complemented controls
are handled by the corresponding signed phases. The constant term
contributes a global phase for each fixed classical prefix. Coherent
prefix selection must retain any relative phase.

For the cheapest Keccak sample, the data computation has 32,703 AND
nodes, 98,360 XOR nodes and AND-depth 23. Its product is 3,014,449.
This grants the attacker all frontier phases, fanout and cleanup for
free in the reported proxy. Counting each frontier phase as one gate
and including one phase layer gives the larger proxy 3,533,928.

The public counter generates all nine phase rows and validates both
the frontier parity and the actual signed-control CZ/Z expression on
144 hash cases. Independent parity validation covered 432 hashes.
Its public main entrypoint was replayed with the ledger write
intercepted and regenerated the final JSON byte for byte. Further
diagonal circuit resynthesis belongs to the broader circuit-optimization
assumption. The assessed phase implementation supports the chosen
2^20 price with the stated margin.

5. Relation to published quantum circuits

The full-symbolic Keccak counter gives 38,400 AND nodes at depth 24
and 115,200 XOR nodes. This agrees with the published AND count and
depth of Haener and Soeken. Their temporary-AND construction gives
153,600 T gates, T-depth 24 and width 46,400 [2]. Measured cleanup can
erase AND work at zero additional T cost [3]. The native audit
therefore avoids automatic T-count doubling for a clean XOR oracle.

Amy et al. report a generic one-block SHA layout with 1,050,224 listed
logical gates and depth 528,768. Their T-optimized layout has more
total logical gates. Their Keccak layout has 34,974,805 listed gates
and depth 11,040 [4]. These published constructions provide a
cross-check on realistic hash costs. The native fixed-prefix and
projection audit supplies the implementation-specific comparison.

6. Classical preprocessing, memory and sharing

A complete fixed-seal component table contains 68,182,605,856 bytes,
approximately 63.5 GiB. A complete hybrid table contains
136,365,211,712 bytes, approximately 127 GiB, before addresses,
lookup workspace and error correction.

For a fixed rooted prefix, the ideal chance that its complete nonce
table contains a bad query response is at most p*kappa_query,
approximately 2^-123.952411382. Classical enumeration charges those
evaluations when the table is constructed. Once values are classically
known, reversible reads can use the recorded table without new oracle
calls. The existing protected-prefix condition charges attempts to
attach cold response rows to later prefixes through the recorded-label
events. This gives a scoped treatment of complete classical tables.

Partial and adaptive tables need joint accounting of preparation,
fresh coherent calls and retained information. Quantum-prepared tables
need their coherent preparation and subsequent reuse costed together.
The physical k_s in the theorem remains the physical query count.

SELECT-SWAP constructions trade workspace, T-count and depth [5].
Parallel lookup circuits can share substantial work across requests
[6]. More recent architectures add further space, depth and error
tradeoffs [7]. The broader attack assessment must therefore cost
complete batches, including coherent prefix selection, shared hash
work, routing, fanout, memory size and cleanup. A unit-cost QRAM model
needs an AES reference evaluated in the same model.

7. Classification and continuing research conditions

The completed direct-circuit assessment supports conservative
preliminary NIST-style Category 1. Native fixed-prefix specialization,
the grind projection and two one-bit phase implementations retain a
resource margin over the chosen price. The exact QROM theorem gives
the displayed complete advantage bound whenever every adversarial
batch meets its stated price. The classical proof layer has a
substantial margin even at unit call cost.

The broader classification uses the standard cryptanalytic condition
that alternative attacks do not lower the resource requirement below
the Category 1 reference. Continue evaluating additional prefix values,
restricted-domain optimization, alternate hash architectures, diagonal
phase synthesis, cross-candidate work sharing and quantum memory
tradeoffs. Keep setup and physical calls in the complete accounting.

The separate authority-preimage, joint HKDF and private-generator
advantages must satisfy the stated margin for the complete extractor.
Each deployment must establish the host conditions in authorization.txt.
