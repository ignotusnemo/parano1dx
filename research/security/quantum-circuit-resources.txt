Published quantum hash circuit resource assessment
9 November 2026

Published full-round circuits give concrete calibration for the selected
response price c=2^20 under logical Clifford+T accounting. The smallest
reported T-count times T-depth projection reviewed here is 3,686,400 for
generic Keccak-f[1600]. This is 3.515625 times the selected price. Applying
that comparison to the native protocol requires fixed-prefix, projected-
output and amortized batch costing in the same gate library.

The existing complete ideal bound is 0.325523273624783 at GD=2^170 and
c=2^20. Its sufficient integer price is 682672. The applicable condition
is, for every coherent batch s,

    sum_j k_sj*c_j <= A_s*delta_s, with c_j >= 2^20.

Here A_s counts elementary logical gates and delta_s is logical depth.
The model grants the complete 512-bit SHA256||Keccak256 response for
the cheaper component's cost. The source densities are approximately
2^-154.94109606926583 and 2^-176.20343729420085. The combined density is
approximately 2^-154.94109549571493. These values and the complete bound
come from security-evaluation.json. Construction costs provide attack
implementations and engineering calibration. A native batch price must
be assessed with the specializations and sharing available to an attack.

NIST's cited evaluation page uses bounded-depth attack costing. It lists
AES-128 key search as 2^170/MAXDEPTH quantum gates and considers depth
values 2^40, 2^64 and 2^96. The comparison here uses that reference budget
as fixed by resource-contract.txt. The assessment follows its concrete
attack-cost approach. A theorem about minimum circuit complexity is a
separate form of evidence. [NIST]

Native block counts and specialization

The actual typed response lengths yield these padded block counts.
SHA-256 uses ceil((L+9)/64). Keccak-256 uses floor(L/136)+1. [SHA, K]

    Bytes       SHA-256       Keccak-256
      623          10               5
      192           4               2
     9036         142              67
      364           6               3
      128           3               1
      128           3               1
      128           3               1
       96           2               1
       68           2               1

The 68-byte query is seal512||nonce32. Its 64-byte seal is fixed for a
classical transcript. SHA-256 can cache the chaining state after the
first block. Its online compression has one variable message word, then
0x80000000, thirteen zero words and the bit length 544. The nonce is a
canonical element of p=2130706433, so at most 31 bits vary. Constant
propagation can also simplify the schedule and early rounds. Digest
feed-forward must be included when producing an XOR response.

Keccak-256 uses one 1088-bit rate block and one 24-round permutation.
SHA3-256 uses the same permutation and rate with delimiter 0x06.
Native Keccak-256 uses delimiter 0x01. The generic permutation topology
transfers, with changed constant preparation. Constant propagation and
output pruning require a new circuit for the native function. [K]

For a superposition of transcript prefixes, the cached SHA state also
needs a coherent preparation or lookup implementation. Cost that access
and its cleanup. Fixed classical prefixes may be amortized across nonce
trials belonging to that transcript.

Published circuit figures

The following are quoted construction counts. T and T-dagger gates are
combined. Full depth and T-depth are distinct. Width means logical
qubits. Neither physical-qubit-time nor surface-code cycle estimates
are substituted into the logical gate-depth budget.

Amy et al, 2016, Tables 1 and 2. [A]

    Transform          Qubits    T count   T-depth   Full gates  Full depth
    SHA-256             2402     401584     171552     1050224      528768
    SHA-256 T-opt       2402     228992      70400     4611328      830720
    Keccak-f            3200     591360        792    34030165       10128
    Keccak-f T-opt      3200     499200        432    34974805       11040

The SHA figures cover a generic 512-bit compression core, 64 rounds and
48 schedule steps. The Keccak figures cover all 24 rounds. Full gates
sum X, P, Z, H, CNOT and T-family counts. The SHA T optimization increases
the full gate-depth product. Preserve both trade-offs when minimizing
that metric. The unoptimized SHA product is 555324844032, about 2^39.015.
The unoptimized Keccak product is 344657511120, about 2^38.326.
Their SHA Grover predicate uses two evaluations plus an
8108-T comparator, giving 466092 T gates. The diffusion adds 8076 T.

    Keccak-f             46400      153600        24            3686400

    SHA-256 low depth   458974     1801788      1519         2736915972

Table 1 gives 38400 ANDs and AND-depth 24 for full Keccak. This is one
nonlinear layer per round. The mapping uses four T gates to compute an
AND and zero T gates for measured uncomputation. Copying inputs to permit
parallel gates uses extra qubits and CNOTs. Full logical gate count and
full depth are absent from these tables. Their T product is partial
accounting for the cited implementation in the Clifford+T library.

These are full 24-round in-place permutation designs for a one-block
message. Stars include AND and AND-dagger operations. Z4 and Z5 use
measurement-based cleanup. The four linear steps require CNOT or X,
whose complete counts and full depth are absent from Table 2. The
optimization software is reported as unavailable publicly.

Kim, Jeong and Han, 2018, Section 6.3 and Table 6, give a SHA-C6 forward
Toffoli-depth of 10112 using 938 qubits. Their Grover iteration explicitly
costs twice the hash plus C256NOT comparison and C266NOT diffusion.
Their comparison changes when parallel workspaces and comparator
ancillae change. [KHJ]

Clean XOR response costing

For a unitary implementation U that preserves x and computes the exact
function into workspace, compute, copy q output bits, then apply U's
inverse. A direct schedule costs 2g+q gates and depth 2d+1. Preparation,
input duplication and missing digest feed-forward add their own costs.
Its T count and T-depth are twice U's values before further optimization.
These formulas describe a particular wrapper.

The logic-network method gives a cheaper cleanup option. Meuli et al
explicitly construct

    |x>|y>|0^k> -> |x>|y XOR f(x)>|0^k>

with at most four T gates per AND. Computation costs four T gates and
measured uncomputation costs zero T gates. The input is preserved.
Output copying uses CNOTs. Therefore a clean XOR realization does not
require doubling the four-T-per-AND count. Measurement, correction,
reset, fanout and output copying still contribute to full accounting.
The construction is defined on superpositions and entangled inputs. [M]

For Lee's unitary Keccak Z2, an explicit compute-copy-inverse wrapper
has 660480 T gates and T-depth 432, a T-only product of 285327360.
Measurement-assisted circuits need their own cleanup schedule. A source
that already includes cleanup should be charged once.

Metric and batch interpretation

In a fixed Clifford+T implementation, the T-only product is no larger
than that implementation's full gate count times full depth. This
comparison applies to that implementation and gate vocabulary. With
atomic AND gates, the Haener-Soeken network's AND-only product is
38400*24=921600. It is 0.87890625 times 2^20 and 1.3499894532074 times
682672. Its Clifford+T T-only product is four times larger. Fixing the
elementary gate vocabulary is therefore material near the chosen price.

For k disjoint copies of one circuit with g gates and depth d,
A=k*g and delta=d. Then A*delta/k=g*d. Serial reuse of one workspace has
A=k*g and delta=k*d, giving A*delta/k=k*g*d before sharing optimizations.
Common subexpressions, retained intermediate states, pipelining and
coherent lookup can change those equalities. Assess the actual batch.

The full hybrid at the cheaper component's cost is an explicit
conservative grant. Output projections can lower comparator and copy
costs. They can also remove gates from the output dependency cone.
Prune before counting. Assess both components and the cheapest useful
projection. Use the resulting price in the complete success evaluator.

Source reconciliation

Haener-Soeken Table 3 quotes an earlier Keccak T count 24640 and T-depth
33. Those values equal Amy's single-round chi plus chi-inverse counts.
Use full permutation rows for a 24-round query. [A, H]

Song et al, 2023, Table 3 reports chi T-depth 23 and full depth 12.
These figures require distinct gate conventions or a correction before
a Clifford+T gate-depth comparison. Its mixed-depth row is preserved
in the JSON notes and excluded from the selected comparison. [S]

The live ePrint metadata for 2024/513 says withdrawn on 26 February 2025.
Indexed preprint tables are excluded. Verify tables in the published
successor, DOI 10.1109/TETC.2025.3546648, before relying on them. [W]

Remaining work

Compile the native fixed-prefix nonce functions with constant propagation
and the actual canonical nonce domain. Prune output projections and
useful acceptance predicates. Record complete logical gate count, full
depth, qubits and measurement costs for meaningful workspace trade-offs.
Check coherent transcript selection and amortized batches against the
stated resource condition. Reevaluate the complete success bound for
any selected price. The parent assessment records native specialization
experiments separately from this literature ledger.

Reproduce arithmetic

The JSON contains exact source counts, all gate-count components,
products, fractions and source locators. This read-only Python snippet
recomputes the products and padded block counts.

    import json
    from pathlib import Path
    d = json.loads(Path('research/security/quantum-circuit-resources.json').read_text())
    for r in d['circuits']:
        if r['gate_counts'] is not None and r['full_depth'] is not None:
            g = sum(r['gate_counts'].values())
            print(r['id'], g, r['full_depth'], g*r['full_depth'])
        if r['T_count'] is not None:
            print(r['id'], 'T-only', r['T_count']*r['T_depth'])
    for r in d['native_block_counts']:
        n = r['input_bytes']
        print(n, (n+72)//64, n//136+1)
